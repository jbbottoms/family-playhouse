How anyone measured Saturn's day at all: the rings heard the planet ring.

Saturn rings like a bell. A mode with m lobes sweeps its pattern round at one speed. Where ring particles orbit at exactly m/(m+1) of that speed, they pile into a spiral wave. One mode, one mark, one radius.

Kepler orbits around GM = 3.793×1016 m³/s². Outer Lindblad resonance. C ring spans 74,658 to 92,000 km; Saturn's cloud tops at 60,268. The modes sweep faster than the planet spins (about 4.6 h for the m=2 mark at W84.64 against a 10.6 h day), which surprised me; radii are real.

Real marks, all m=2, read by Cassini watching stars blink through the ring: W76.44, W84.64, W87.19 (km ×1000). Twenty-odd waves in all.

From the notes: bulk day 10h 33m 38s, and a fuzzy core, rock and ice smeared out to 60% of the radius, about 17 Earths of it. Mankovich & Fuller 2021.
